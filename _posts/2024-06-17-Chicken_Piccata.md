Ingredients:

* big package of chicken breast. I think it's somewhere around 3\-4 lbs? I suppose I should actually pay attention some day
* \~32 oz chicken stock or broth, but have more on hand if needed
* All purpose flour for dredging chicken
* enough lemons for 2 cups of juice
* big jar of capers (I think 16 oz)
* unsalted butter
* olive oil
* One heavy tablespoon of minced garlic

Mike's Points:

* I use WAY more capers than any of the recipes call for.  I think I use a whole 16 ounce jar, drained, rinsed and soaked in cold water for a bit.
* I use roughly 16 ounces lemon juice to 32 ounces of chicken broth / stock.  Then, if needed, I'll dilute it with more broth / stock
* The butter and olive oil amount is pretty loose, but you'll want to add in equal amounts.  By the end, you'll want to at least use a whole stick of butter.
* Lately I've been pre\-cutting the chicken to bite\-size pieces after frying.  Not as great for presentation, but makes it convenient to eat.

Instructions:

Chicken:

1. Start by trimming off any nasty, stringy or fatty bits off the chicken breast.  Then, slice in half like butterflying.  Pound them thin enough to break down tough spots, but not so thin that there's nothing holding them together.
2. Dredge the chicken in flour
3. Start with a few tablespoons of both olive oil and butter (equal amounts) in a sauce pot.  Fry the chicken until sorta browned on each side, and set aside.  We're going to simmer later, so cooking through isn't necessarily important.
4. Add more olive oil and butter as needed.
5. Cook the rest of the chicken and set aside.  If you're pre\-cutting, do that while the sauce heats.

Sauce:1. In VERY quick succession, add the garlic to the oil/butter frying mixture and VERY quickly after, add the lemon juice.  The garlic will fry very quickly, but can also overcook and get bitter quickly.
2. After adding the lemon juice, scrape any bits off the bottom of the pan.
3. Add the capers and chicken broth/stock.
4. Bring to a simmer.
5. Add the cooked chicken to the sauce mixture and simmer for 5 mins.  If you DIDN'T cut the chicken, take it out and put it aside.
6. Taste the sauce for flavor.  If too acidic, add additional stock/broth.  I very rarely add salt or pepper.  The flour from the chicken usually thickens the sauce some, but you CAN add a corn starch slurry if needed, to thicken further.  I think I've only done that once.

If you timed it right, the pasta finished right about the same time as the sauce.  Drain the pasta and toss with some of the sauce to keep from sticking.
